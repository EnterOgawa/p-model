Abstract

This paper (Part II) is the astrophysical validation volume of the P-model. Assuming the mapping
frozen in Part I (P → ϕ → gravity/clocks/light) and the shared parameter β, it fixes reproducible
comparison metrics from public primary data. The validation logic is condensed into the validation
summary, and each topic is written in the order input (primary source) → process (fixed conditions)
→ metric → figure → notes (systematics), so that a third party can reach the same conclusion from
the same inputs.
This paper does not claim a fit in which β is adjusted to match the observations. Instead, under
the frozen mapping, it states explicit differential predictions (for example, the coeﬀicient difference
in EHT and the cosmological distance mapping), and fixes as output the falsification conditions that
specify which observation rejects the model once which threshold is crossed. The velocity-saturation
parameter δ0 is not part of the Part I core. Here it is organized only as an extension hypothesis together
with its upper bounds and rejection conditions. Within the early thermal history, BBN is fixed in
Part III (the quantum volume) as raw watch and, after propagated derivation uncertainty, pass for
the minimal He-4 chain (Yp ≈ 0.25). It is therefore referenced here as a boundary condition rather
than re-derived. The acceptance or rejection of the full multi-isotope consistency (D/H, He-3/He-4,
Li-7) is governed by the Watch verdict in Part III.

1 Introduction

The role of this paper (Part II) is to follow the Part I reference document (symbol conventions,
minimum assumptions, mappings, and frozen values) and fix the validation procedures so that public
primary data in astrophysics reproduce ”the same input → the same output.” The targets span weak
fields (the Solar System), dynamical phenomena (binary radiation and gravitational-wave chirps),
strong fields (EHT), and cosmological scales. This paper, however, does not immediately conclude
that the reference framework has been replaced by a final theory. In cosmology in particular, the target
is the late-universe audit of SNe Ia/BAO/AP/CMB peaks. Derivations on the early-thermal-history
side (the minimal He-4 chain in BBN and the blackbody origin of the CMB) are separated into Part
III (the quantum volume), and are referenced here only as boundary conditions. Likewise, the growth-
rate mapping for structure formation (RSD-derived f σ8 ) is fixed here only in its initial minimal form
including delayed response. The current verdict is limited to the three low-redshift BOSS DR12
consensus points (z = 0.38, 0.51, 0.61), while the final decision across the full growth history, including
the high-redshift side, is treated as a separate audit task.
What this paper fixes are the definitions of observables, processing conditions (inputs, preprocessing,
windows, and outlier rules), and metrics (including the decomposition of uncertainty). The conclu-
sions are then reduced to a validation summary and falsification-condition packs that can be tracked
mechanically. This policy foregrounds falsifiability, namely where the decision is made, rather than
the strength of the claim. Future improvements should clarify, under the same metrics, whether statis-
tics (covariance) or systematics (procedure dependence) dominate, and then connect that result to
required precision estimates and observational proposals.
This trilogy is meant to be read in the order Part I → Part II → Part III. Part I establishes the
mappings, freezing rules, and falsification form; Part II accumulates application tests from classical
to cosmological scales under the same framework; and Part III treats the quantum domain with the
same interface, so that the P-model can be assessed not as a ”claim” but as a falsifiable validation
program.
The approach of this study is not to modify or extend the parameters of the standard cosmological
model (ΛCDM). Instead, it deductively applies the P-model-specific assumptions established in Part
I, including the minimal model of the static background P, and compares the resulting consequences
with observational data. The standard theory is used only as a comparison target in the interpretation
of distance indicators and black-hole shadows, not as the theoretical foundation.
Style rule: the main text closes using only the P-model’s own definitional mapping procedure. Com-
parisons with reference frameworks (existing theories) are isolated in reference notes at the ends of
sections and are not allowed to replace definitions or derivations in the main text.
Reference note: when this paper refers to GR or the standard cosmology as comparison rulers, the
grammatical subject remains the P-model throughout. The reference frameworks are restricted to the
comparison of final expressions of results.

3 Data and Methods

This paper starts from primary sources (the observations themselves or oﬀicial public datasets), and
fixes both the storage of the acquired data and the processing conditions (windows, outlier rules, and
so on) together with the output filenames. The primary sources (URLs and access dates) are collected
in the appendix ”Data Sources (Primary Sources).” This structure allows one to track ”which output
came from which input” even when data-provider updates or environment differences exist.
Some public primary sources do contain preprocessing based on the standard theory (geodetic systems,
time systems, derived products, and so on). Therefore, at the present stage, this paper does not
claim full raw-data reanalysis. Priority is given to fixing the inputs (primary sources) and visualizing
corrections and systematics through fixed outputs.
The metrics are condensed into the validation summary, and each section states under which inputs,
processing conditions, and uncertainties (statistical/systematic) the result was obtained. Statistical
uncertainty is evaluated by fixed procedures such as bootstrap, while systematics are treated separately
as sensitivities to procedural knobs such as window choice, offsets, and model selection. The key point
is not to move β afterward in order to force agreement, but to keep it frozen as defined in Part I and,
under that frozen value, output differential predictions and rejection thresholds.

3.1 Shared Rejection Procedure

Each validation block in this paper fixes its falsifiability through the following common form. From
this point onward, every section follows this order, so that the content functions not as loose discussion
but as a reproducible and rejectable protocol.
Purpose: what is validated or discriminated in the section. Input: the primary source (or a derived
public product), its origin, time span, version, and the local fixed storage path. Process: prepro-
cessing (selection, outlier rules, windows, offsets) and frozen parameters. Equation: the equations
used in the section (definitions of variables only; no derivations). Metric: the statistics adopted (for
example, RMS, �², z) and, if needed, the rejection threshold (for example, beyond 3�). Result: the
principal numbers, with stat and sys separated. Representative figures are inserted when needed,
with 2-4 lines stating what the reader should look at. Discussion: interpretation of the result, includ-
ing alternative explanations. Notes: the reference framework (GR, and so on), dominant systematics,
limitations, and the update policy.

3.2 Primary Data and Preprocessing Dependence (Explicit Statement of Depen-
    dencies)

This paper claims a primary-source-first workflow, but public astrophysical data often contain practical
preprocessing. To avoid overstatement, the dependence points are stated first, together with the inputs
that would be needed to remove those dependencies.

 • GPS (IGS products): the default inputs are IGS precise orbit/clock products (SP3/CLK and
   related files), which already arise from an estimation procedure based on raw observation data
   (RINEX observations from each station). To remove preprocessing dependence, one would need
   to fix the observation RINEX files together with the network model and the estimation procedure
   (weights, outliers, and corrections). At the present stage, priority is given instead to visualizing
   the dependency points themselves.

 • Cassini (DSN tracking; PDS TDF): TDF is close to a primary tracking observable, but
   can still include operational processing such as frequency generation, time-scale handling, and
   ground-station corrections. Removing the dependency would require raw receiver-side logs and a
   fully fixed correction pipeline (time scales, media corrections, antenna motion, and so on).

 • LLR (CRD Normal Point): the Normal Point is a compressed product of the raw photon
   sequence. Removing preprocessing dependence would require the full-rate photon time series and
   the fixed Normal Point construction rules (binning and outlier treatment).

 • EHT (black-hole shadow): the default inputs are measured quantities published in papers and
   public tables, which already include preprocessing from calibration, imaging, and model fitting.
   Removing that dependency would require public visibility data together with fixed calibration
   procedures and imaging knobs.

Process: atmospheric delay, tides (solid Earth + lunar body), and ocean loading are added step

a chi-square-like indicator. Auxiliary metrics include the median RMS over station×reflector and the

7 Conclusion

P-model is a framework that uniformly expresses gravity, clocks, and light propagation through the
time-wave density P . In this paper, we established a reproducible validation pipeline for representative
cases ranging from weak fields to strong fields and cosmological scales, and presented the entry points
for both the differential prediction (the EHT coeﬀicient difference) and the upper-bound constraint
on the extension hypothesis (velocity saturation δ0 ).
In cosmology, by generating ξ0 /ξ2 + cov directly from the DESI DR1 raw products and performing
the BAO ϵ fit, we confirmed that tension across multiple tracers appears stably in pbg coordinates.
However, this paper does not immediately interpret that result as a rejection of the P-model. Instead,
it quantifies the result as a reconnection condition based on the premise structure of the distance
indicators (�, sL , sR ; Δ�), and fixes it as a falsifiable prediction.
Regarding DDR (distance duality), this paper explicitly treats the large discrepancy with the P-model
minimum as a serious issue, and organizes it as a preliminary audit of which (1+z) factor is embedded
in the distance-estimation I/F. Furthermore, the correction introduced in the preliminary audit must
be applied under the same conditions to the ΛCDM side as well, so that the comparison remains fair.
On that basis, we adopt the P-model-specific index η (P ) together with the rejection condition of 3�
across multiple datasets.
For the DM alternative branch, we fixed a mechanism that explains both the rotational support of
galaxies and the center-of-mass separation in colliding systems by the same equation, through the
SPARC gP correction and the delayed response of the Bullet-type systems. On the other hand, the
ISW / weak-lensing side remains at the prediction-fixed stage, and the simultaneous fit to the CMB
full Cℓ and weak-lensing statistics has not yet been completed. Therefore, the overall verdict for the
DM alternative branch remains Watch.
For the derivation of � in the weak-field regime, the cross-channel audit in Part IV is treated as the
current oﬀicial verdict. In the LLR decontaminated branch, β = 1.000674 ± 0.000961 is consistent
with the theoretical prediction β = 1, with abs(z(β − 1)) = 0.702. By contrast, the cross consistency
with VLBI fails at abs(z(βvlbi − βllr )) = 13.392, so the VLBI comparator eligibility remains fixed at
false. However, because the LLR bias / promotion gate and the messenger pair gate have been cleared
and the promotion conditions of policy_D have been confirmed, the active policy has already been
switched to policy_D_exclude_plus_messenger_fusion. Accordingly, the terminal verdict is fixed at
beta_terminal=Pass.
From here onward, the priority is to isolate the conditions under which differential predictions become
observationally discriminable, that is, the factors that dominate the uncertainty, and to reduce the
strong-field and cosmological falsification routes to more direct observational proposals. The cosmo-
logical conclusion of this paper focuses on the observational audit of the late universe, while the BBN
side has already fixed the minimal He-4 chain (Yp ≈ 0.25) in Part III (the quantum volume) as raw
