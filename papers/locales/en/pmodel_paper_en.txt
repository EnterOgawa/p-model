Abstract

This paper (Part I) fixes only three items as the reference baseline of the P-model. (i) Mapping:
from the scalar limit of time-wave density, P (x), and its covariant extension Pµ = (Pt , P1 , P2 , P3 ), we
define the potential ϕ and fix the main equations that map it to the observables of gravity, clocks,
light propagation, and rotational effects, including the sign convention. (ii) Freezing: the light-
propagation parameter β is not fitted ad hoc but derived as the theoretical prediction β = 1 from the
null geodesics of the effective metric (1A.1), and is managed in three layers together with its operational
frozen value and terminal audit status (3.4). Parts II and III do not change this value locally; if an
update becomes necessary, Part I is revised and all downstream outputs are regenerated from here.
(iii) Falsification conditions: for each observable treated in Parts II and III, we define a machine-
readable falsification pack that fixes, under this mapping, which statistic must exceed which threshold
for rejection. In addition, the frozen action skeleton defined here provides the theoretical anchor points
on the quantum side that are audited in Parts III-A/B: the transverse photon branch, the selector-
law anchor of the fine-structure constant, and the coupled-localization anchor for the W/Z boson
masses. Their derivation and acceptance criteria are governed by Parts III-A/B. In this manuscript,
  closed means mathematical closure, not truth of the theory or eventual observational acceptance.
Individual application tests in the Solar System, dynamical phenomena, strong-field gravity, cosmology,
and quantum physics are collected in Parts II and III; here they are referenced only as needed.

From this point onward, the subject is always the P-model. The mappings, freezing rules, and rejection

Definition of an effective model: in this paper, an ”effective model” is a framework in which the
action principle (Lagrangian) is defined in minimal form, the mapping to observables is fixed first, and
consistency with primary data is then tested under that mapping. It is therefore distinct from pure
phenomenology because it can present falsifiable predictions, namely explicit rejection conditions.
Definition of ”closed”: in this paper, ”closed” means mathematical closure in the sense that the
P-model side has already supplied the action skeleton, interaction dictionary, mappings to observables,
and falsification conditions across the major domains of modern physics. This does not mean that
the theory is true or that it will eventually be accepted by future observations. P-model v2.0 is the
version that fixes this mathematical closure and leaves its empirical acceptance or rejection to public
primary data and future observations.

 • Adopted effective sector: the derivation of the theoretical origin remains open, but the obser-
